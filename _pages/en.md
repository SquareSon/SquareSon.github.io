<h1 id="about" class="section-title">About Me</h1>

<p class="page__lead">I am Zi Fang, a Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at the Robotics Institute, Shanghai Jiao Tong University. My research spans <strong>3D perception, embodied intelligence, and medical robotics</strong>. I study how imperfect medical images and multi-sensor observations can become continuous, trustworthy 3D worlds for robotic decision-making.</p>

<p>My doctoral research focuses on ultrasound-guided thyroid puncture. It connects freehand ultrasound observation, continuous 3D reconstruction, multi-sweep spatial alignment, tissue–instrument semantic modeling, and a navigation coordinate chain. I am interested in algorithms that remain meaningful when brought into calibration, phantom experiments, and robotic prototypes.</p>

<ul class="research-keywords" aria-label="Research keywords">
  <li>3D Perception</li><li>Embodied Intelligence</li><li>Medical Robotics</li><li>Freehand Ultrasound</li><li>Neural Fields</li><li>Robot Navigation</li>
</ul>

<div class="notice"><strong>Evidence boundary:</strong> current public results support conclusions at method, module, and phantom-prototype levels. Component calibration residuals must not be interpreted as end-to-end puncture accuracy, and the materials do not establish clinical safety or effectiveness.</div>

  <section>
    <h2>Embodied Spatial Intelligence</h2>
    <p>Unifying images, poses, continuous scene representations, and tissue–instrument geometry into spatial coordinates that robots can interpret and act upon.</p>

    <h2>Implicit 3D thyroid ultrasound reconstruction and puncture navigation</h2>
    <p class="paper-meta">Ph.D. dissertation · Shanghai Jiao Tong University · 2026</p>
    <p>The system integrates freehand ultrasound, stereo near-infrared tracking, probe and needle-tip calibration, continuous 3D fields, tissue and needle semantics, and a navigation workbench.
